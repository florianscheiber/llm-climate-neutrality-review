Macro-Economic Cost

Besides a carbon price, how do the reviewed studies express the economy-wide cost of getting to net zero? Three metrics turn up — GDP impact, total system cost, investment need — and each is reported by only a handful of papers, in forms that barely compare.

The percentage change in a study's total energy- or power-system cost between a decarbonisation scenario and its own reference. The reference is not the same thing across studies — a BAU run, an unconstrained least-cost design, another net-zero variant — so the sign is more meaningful than the level. A dedicated-CCS or constrained-technology scenario is where the large positive values come from; several studies find the transition scenario is cheaper than their reference.

Method

  • Panel A — harmonized-costs.csv rows with Cost_Type = "(Change in) GDP" and a % value whose label names a GDP loss / change / impact against a reference (growth rates, per-capita and absolute GDP levels excluded). A "loss" quoted as a positive magnitude is negated. One value per scenario, at the last reported year.
  • Panel B — rows in System cost (total) or Other whose label names a system / discounted / energy-production cost and a relative comparison. % is used directly; a ratio (1.014) becomes +1.4%; "reduction" / "saving" phrasings are signed negative. One value per scenario — the largest-magnitude change.
  • Panel C — Investment cost (total) rows for an economy- or energy-system-wide total (single-technology and single-sector lines dropped). USD2025/yr is used as is; USD2025 is treated as cumulative and divided by the span of its Year range (start defaults to 2020). One value per scenario — the widest-scope figure.
  • Net-zero flag from netzero-flags.csv; many of these scenarios are carbon-neutral by construction (the metric is "relative to BAU") but were not flagged because no emission time series was extracted, so the flag is shown, not used as a filter.

Caveats

  • This is the thinnest analysis in the pipeline — 3, 6 and 7 papers per panel. Do not read a distribution into any of it.
  • The three panels are not the same scenarios and mostly not the same papers.
  • "Reference scenario" means something different in every study (Panels A, B).
  • Panel C pools whole-economy and power-only investment, and a cumulative-to-annual conversion assumes a flat profile.
  • GDP-loss estimates are model-structure artefacts as much as findings — CGE vs partial-equilibrium framing dominates.
