Mitigation Contribution at Net Zero

Mitigation options are reported in units that don't compare — MW, MWh, MtCO₂/yr, percentages. Two within-scenario fractions make them comparable: carbon handled as a share of baseline emissions, and each energy source as a share of low-carbon supply.

What it shows

  • Carbon management is modest per route. The largest single carbon-management contribution (BECCS) is a median 22% of baseline emissions (21 scenarios); the routes below it sit between 4% and 15%. No single removal or capture route carries the transition on its own.
  • The low-carbon supply mix is solar-led. Other renewables is a median 44% of reported low-carbon generation. Solar and Wind together are a median 47% of it.
  • The spread within every row is wide — scenarios disagree far more about how much each option contributes than about which options appear.

Method

1 net-zero filter
→
2 option rows at the end year
→
3 normalise per scenario
  • Net-zero membership from netzero-flags.csv (is_netzero == True) — the same filter as the other figures. 203 scenarios qualify.
  • Panel A. harmonized-mitigation.csv rows for CCS / CDR / CCU with a clean MtCO2[eq][/yr] value. CDR is split by Subsector_std / detail into BECCS, DACCS, afforestation & land, other. Cumulative-stock rows are dropped; annual-flow rows win where both exist. Contribution = |option MtCO2/yr| / first_emissions_Mt from the flag — a size-free share of the emissions the pathway had to eliminate. Shares above 300% of baseline (1 rows) are dropped as a mis-matched denominator.
  • Panel B. Rows reporting generation in MWh[/yr] by source (Solar, Wind, Hydro, Bioenergy, Geothermal, Nuclear, Hydrogen / synfuel, other). A scenario is kept only if it names ≥ 2 sources at the end year; each source's share is of the scenario's summed low-carbon generation.
  • End year = the latest year the scenario reports the option, capped at the flag's net-zero year.

Caveats

  • Panel A leans toward carbon management. Renewables, efficiency and electrification are mostly reported in MW / MWh, not MtCO₂, so their CO₂ contribution is rarely stated and they barely appear here.
  • Panel A n = 68 scenarios / 22 papers; panel B n = 50 / 18. Small rows (a source or route with n < 3) are a point cloud, not a distribution.
  • CO₂ and CO₂e are pooled; a few absolute MtCO2 rows may be cumulative despite no “cumulative” label, which would inflate their share.
  • Panel B is a share of the reported sources, not of total final energy — a scenario that only lists solar and wind reads as 100% those two even if it also runs nuclear.
  • End years span 2020–2080; the panels are not additive with each other (MWh ≠ MtCO₂).
